Episode title: Turkish Airlines Bets Big on Boeing While China Ghosts Them, Frontier's CEO Points Fingers, and Tulum's Airport Dream Dies
Episode Summary: Turkish Airlines just placed a massive order for the 737 MAX right as Boeing's hopes for a Chinese mega-deal quietly fall apart. Plus, Frontier's CEO blames credit card companies for his airline's struggles, Lufthansa's social media team picks a fight it can't win, and Tulum's brand-new airport turns out to be a very expensive ghost town.
Hey, welcome to Runway Briefing for Thursday, September 24th. Let's get into it.

So, Boeing. There was a lot of hope going into this week's Trump-Xi summit that China would drop a mega-order — like, five hundred jets, the kind of deal that fixes a decade of balance sheet problems in one signature. That's not happening. Sources say it's basically dead, and now Boeing's just trying to hang onto the two hundred jet deal from May that hasn't even been made official yet.

Here's why that stings. China used to be Boeing's biggest growth market, full stop. But after years of tariffs, the 737 MAX grounding, and Beijing cozying up to Airbus and its own homegrown C919, that relationship's basically frozen.

So Boeing needs wins elsewhere, and it got a decent one today. Turkish Airlines just ordered up to 150 737 MAX jets. That's a serious vote of confidence, and it's Turkish doubling down on becoming the connector airline between Europe, Asia, and everywhere in between. Good day for Boeing's order book. Rough day for Boeing's biggest long-term growth story.

Okay, Frontier. Their CEO's been going around blaming, quote, "credit card subsidies" for why ultra-low-cost carriers can't compete anymore. His argument is basically that legacy airlines use co-brand card revenue to subsidize cheap fares, and that's unfair.

Here's the thing though — that's not really what's killing Frontier. Passengers don't want the bare-bones, pay-for-everything experience anymore. That's why Frontier itself just added better seats and Starlink. You don't do that because credit cards are unfair. You do that because customers were leaving for a slightly nicer seat on Delta or American. I get wanting a villain, but sometimes the villain is just changing consumer taste.

Now, a story that's honestly a little sad if you like aviation history. Tulum opened a brand-new airport back in 2023, right next to the Mayan ruins, with a lot of hype about becoming the next big Riviera Maya gateway. It has not worked. Aeromexico just filed to pull out entirely by the end of the month.

Cancún is still the powerhouse — three hundred fifty flights a day, connections practically to Argentina. Tulum was supposed to skim off some of that tourist traffic. Turns out building an airport next to some ruins doesn't automatically create demand. Cranky Flier's been tracking this one, and it's basically a case study in "if you build it, they might just... not come."

Quick one — Lufthansa's social media team had a moment. A passenger going after a payment the airline had already approved months ago finally posted publicly on X. Lufthansa's official response? Basically, "our answer isn't going to magically change just because you're asking on Twitter now." Very cool, very professional.

Except — guess what changed within twenty four hours? The response. Because it went viral and got over a million views. So it very much did magically change, Flo. That's the whole game with airline social media — you can be right about the facts and still lose completely on the internet.

And finally, because I have to share this — a US senator stood up on the Senate floor this week worried that pilots, flight attendants, and air traffic controllers might secretly be furries. His actual quote was "Lord help us, it's terrifying when you think about this." No incident, no safety issue, just vibes. I don't even have a joke better than the thing that actually happened.
