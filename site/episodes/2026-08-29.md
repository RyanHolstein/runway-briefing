Episode Summary: Delta's CEO wants to run with Singapore Airlines and Emirates, JetBlue's new first class comes with a side of shrinking economy legroom, Qantas retires the A380 early, and Nashville might just become "Dolly Parton International."

Hey, welcome to Runway Briefing for Saturday, August 29, 2026. Let's get into it.

So Ed Bastian went on a podcast this week and said he wants Delta mentioned in the same breath as Singapore Airlines and Emirates. Not United, not American — Singapore and Emirates. That's the tier he's shooting for.

And here's the fun part. Scott Kirby said basically the same thing to a reporter in Denver, on the same day, hours apart. Two rival CEOs, independently, both suddenly name-dropping Singapore Airlines like it's the new industry flex.

Is Delta actually there yet? No. Singapore Airlines has been the gold standard for like three decades. But Delta's not crazy to chase it either — the lounges are genuinely great, the tech's improved, people do pay up for the brand. It's just funny watching two American CEOs who spent years fighting each other suddenly agree on the same aspirational airline to steal from. Great minds, or great PR consultants, one of the two.

Alright, JetBlue. Update on something that's been brewing for a while — we finally got a leaked seat map for the new BlueFirst cabin on the A320s. Twelve first class seats, up front, two-by-two, finally giving JetBlue a real domestic first class product like every other airline already has.

Here's the catch. Total seat count on the plane isn't changing. So to fit first class, they're shrinking the EvenMore extra-legroom section from five rows down to three. And word is standard economy pitch is dropping from JetBlue's famous 32 inches down to around 30.

So JetBlue spent twenty years bragging about having the most legroom in coach. And the tradeoff for finally getting first class is... quietly giving that up. Rich getting a little richer, everybody else getting a little more cramped. Very on-brand for 2026 airline strategy, honestly.

Qantas is retiring the A380 four years early — 2028 instead of 2032. They're getting a wave of new A350s and 787s over the next couple years, and with fuel prices where they are in 2026, keeping a four-engine double-decker around just doesn't pencil out anymore.

The A380 was the plane of the future when it launched. Now it's basically the world's most beautiful money pit. Emirates is still all-in on theirs, but everybody else has been sneaking out the back door for years, and Qantas just decided to stop pretending otherwise.

Quick one on customer service — View from the Wing flagged a training banner American Airlines put up for flight attendants. The message: be resilient, de-escalate. Which, sure, fine advice. But it's also kind of telling that the airline's big strategy for improving service is asking employees to just personally absorb more stress, instead of, you know, fixing the actual problems. Feels like putting a "be more patient" sign over a broken air conditioner.

And speaking of flight attendants having a rough summer — Paddle Your Own Kanoo had a nice piece on why cabin crew have been losing their cool lately. Heat, delays, packed planes, and a passenger mix that's just... more entitled since the pandemic. Case in point, also from this week: a Frontier passenger refused to give up someone else's assigned seat and called the flight attendant a "jive turkey." Which, first of all, nobody's said that since 1975. Second of all — buddy, just sit in your seat.

Last one, and it's the one everybody's texting about. Nashville International Airport is looking at renaming itself after Dolly Parton. This came together fast — the Tennessee governor's already talked to her family, they're reportedly open to it. Obviously there'll be a licensing deal involved, same as when Palm Beach became Trump International.

BNA never made sense as a code anyway — it's from the old Berry Field name. Dolly Parton International would actually be a massive upgrade. Genuinely can't think of a better fit for a Nashville airport. Nine to Five plays overhead, you already know you've landed in Tennessee.
