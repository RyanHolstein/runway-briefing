Runway Briefing - Saturday, August 29, 2026
Episode Summary: United has a rough week in the sky and a huge one on paper, Scott Kirby predicts American's exit from Chicago, Frontier reshuffles its loyalty program again, and the FAA can't seem to get controllers a raise.

Hey, welcome to Runway Briefing for Saturday, August 29th. Let's get into it.

So first up — if you were flying United from LA to Newark on Thursday, rough day for you.

Flight 1403 took off, the crew reported an engine issue, and they made an emergency landing in Las Vegas. That part's actually fine — planes divert, that's the system working. Nobody panic.

Except then the replacement aircraft United sent to finish the trip also ran into trouble. Same route, second diversion. One flight number, two separate "sorry folks, we need another plane" moments.

Passengers eventually got to Newark, just many hours and one existential crisis later. United hasn't said much beyond "safety first," which, sure — but somewhere there's a gate agent in Vegas who deserves a raise.

Speaking of United, they had themselves a week. At a big reveal in Newark, they announced ten new international routes — eight of them to cities no other US airline even flies to. Places like Luxembourg, Ljubljana, and Marseille, mostly out of Newark, because apparently everyone there is desperate to get to the south of France.

And the new Airbus A321XLR — this fancy long-range jet built to cross oceans — is making its debut this September. Not overseas. Not yet. Its first job is Dulles to San Diego. So United's brand-new international workhorse is starting its career doing a puddle-jump to Southern California before it gets promoted to Europe in December. Feels like hiring a Formula 1 driver and having them run your carpool line for a few months first.

American had a route announcement too — Porto, Reykjavik, Vienna — solid additions, but a much quieter splash next to United's fireworks.

Which brings us to the drama of the week. United CEO Scott Kirby is once again saying American is basically doomed at O'Hare. He's telling Crain's Chicago Business that American lost over a billion dollars there last year, and that "economic gravity" is eventually going to force American out entirely — leaving United to turn O'Hare into a fortress hub with a thousand daily flights.

Kirby's said versions of this before, so take it with a grain of salt — he does have a bit of a vested interest in American leaving. But if he's right, that's a lot less competition, and a lot less reason for fares out of Chicago to stay friendly.

Okay, Frontier. They're doing that thing again where they give with one hand and take with the other. The good news — Elite Diamond status just got easier, dropping from 100,000 points needed to 75,000.

The bad news — if you're not carrying the Frontier credit card or you like booking basic fares, your mileage earning just got cut by up to 90 percent. Oh, and Platinum members just lost unlimited companion travel. So, faster path to the top, smaller ladder to climb, fewer reasons to actually want to be up there. Classic Frontier.

Last one — the FAA. Congress approved a 2.8 percent raise for air traffic controllers back in April. It's now almost September, and controllers still don't have it. Meanwhile FAA administrator Bryan Bedford is out there talking about "reform" while controllers are pulling ten-hour shifts, six days a week.

And this lands right after reports that two controllers left early the night of the LaGuardia crash, with the FAA reportedly moving to fire both of them. So the system's stretched thin, the people running it are exhausted, and the raise everyone agreed on months ago is still stuck somewhere in a drawer. Not exactly confidence-inspiring.
