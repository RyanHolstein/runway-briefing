Scott Kirby Calls It a "Betrayal," Two Jets Get Fighter Escorts, and Lufthansa Retires a Legend
Episode Summary: United's CEO opens up about getting fired by American, defends charging you for a window seat, and wants a wealth tax — all in one wild interview. Plus, two different flights get intercepted by fighter jets in the same 24 hours, an American Eagle flight evacuates onto the wing in St. Louis, Lufthansa finally retires its last A340, and Wizz Air quietly wipes 20 cities off its map.
Hey, welcome to Runway Briefing for Monday, October 19th. Let's get into it.

So, Scott Kirby gave a pretty wild interview to the New York Times, and there's a lot to unpack here. First, he's defending United's new fees for window and aisle seats. His argument is basically, "some people just want cheap, some people want a better seat, so we charge for the better seat." Fine, except... an aisle seat in economy doesn't actually cost United more to provide. It's the same seat. It's just the one you want. That's not a product upgrade, that's just picking your pocket with extra steps.

Then it gets personal. Kirby says American Airlines promised him the CEO job, then fired him, and he calls it a "betrayal." Which, fair, except American handed him thirteen million dollars on the way out with no non-compete. So Doug Parker kept his job, and American basically funded the guy who'd go build a stronger rival. That's not a betrayal story, that's a self-inflicted wound.

Oh, and he also wants a national wealth tax and inheritance taxes near a hundred percent. From the guy selling four-figure caviar in first class. Make it make sense.

Lufthansa had a big day. They finally retired their last A340-600 — the really long one, with the downstairs bathrooms in economy, remember those? It flew in from Washington Dulles and that was it, twenty-plus years of service, done. Thing is, Lufthansa actually wanted to retire these years ago, but new plane deliveries kept getting delayed, so they had to keep flying four-engine gas guzzlers because they had nothing else with a big enough first class cabin. Now the A350-1000 and more 787s are finally showing up, with the 777-9 next year. And in case you thought Lufthansa was just subtracting planes, they also announced a new U.S. destination starting next May. Out with the old, in with the new, finally.

Okay, weird one. Two separate flights got fighter jet escorts in the same 24 hours, which, I don't love as a trend. An Austrian Airlines flight from Tel Aviv got a bomb threat called in, diverted to Sofia, and got a Bulgarian MiG-29 escort down. They searched the plane, found nothing, everybody eventually got to Vienna. Separately, a British Airways flight from Cyprus to Heathrow went quiet — pilots just didn't check in with German air traffic control — so Germany scrambled two Typhoon jets to go look in the cockpit window and make sure nobody unauthorized was flying the plane. Turned out to be a radio issue, pilots got back on frequency. Both totally fine outcomes, but imagine looking out your window mid-flight and seeing a fighter jet. That's not a travel story you forget.

Closer to home, an American Eagle flight out of St. Louis had to turn around right after takeoff — passengers reported a weird smell, haze in the cabin, oxygen masks dropping — and ended up evacuating onto the wing. One mom on board said it was her kids' first flight ever. Talk about an introduction to aviation. American says a reported odor triggered the return, no fire confirmed, plane's getting inspected. Nobody was hurt, but that's a hell of a story for the group chat.

And last one — Wizz Air just quietly cancelled flights to twenty destinations. This is the budget carrier that's actually been growing, up twenty percent year over year in daily flights across Europe. So this isn't a company in free fall, it's more of a pruning move, cutting underperforming routes to double down on the ones that work. Still, if you're one of those twenty cities, that's cold comfort.
