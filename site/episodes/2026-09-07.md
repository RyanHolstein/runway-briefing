United beats Delta back into Tel Aviv as Middle East service slowly reopens, and the guy who got duct-taped to his American Airlines seat has now lost his job too. Hey, welcome to Runway Briefing for Monday, September 7th, 2026. Let's get into it.

So the big one, and it's a hard one. A Boeing 767 cargo plane, flying for Amazon Prime Air, overran the runway at Miami International yesterday afternoon. It came in from San Juan, landed on runway 30, didn't stop in time, blew through the airport fence, crossed an actual public road, and ended up on fire in a parking lot. At least five people are dead.

Here's the thing that makes this worse — we still don't fully know who those five people are. The plane was operated by a company called 21 Air, flying under Amazon's brand, and reports haven't confirmed if the dead were on board or on the ground when it crashed through that fence. That's the detail everyone's waiting on, because a runway excursion killing its own crew is a tragedy. A runway excursion killing people in cars on a public road is a very different kind of story, and a very different kind of investigation.

Miami went into a full ground stop, Transportation Secretary Sean Duffy confirmed it publicly, and the FAA is investigating. We'll keep tracking this one because cargo carrier safety oversight — especially these charter arrangements where Amazon's logo is on a plane Amazon doesn't actually operate — is about to get a lot more scrutiny.

Okay, on to something a little more hopeful. United is flying back into Tel Aviv starting tomorrow, September 8th, first time since the Iran War kicked off back in February. It's launching as a double-daily from Newark, then adding Chicago and Dulles by late October, and San Francisco next March.

Delta actually beat them to it, restarting JFK to Tel Aviv on Sunday. American, meanwhile, says it's not coming back before early 2027. So you've basically got three US airlines on three completely different timelines for the same market, which tells you a lot about how each of them is reading the security risk. El Al has had this route pretty much to itself for months, and honestly, that monopoly pricing is exactly why United and Delta are racing back the second they feel comfortable. Nobody wants to leave that revenue sitting on the table any longer than they have to.

And in Montreal, a British Airways 787 accidentally deployed an emergency slide on the ground, and it inflated directly onto a catering truck. Nobody was hurt, but somewhere there's a maintenance report that just says "oops" in very official language.
