Delta's Riyadh Gamble Gets Scarier, Southwest Eyes Widebodies, and American Goes All In on Starlink
Episode Summary: Delta's Riyadh launch is two weeks away, missiles are still hitting the airport, and the CEO is telling reporters "safety will make the call" while warning employees not to go. Plus, Southwest might actually buy widebody jets for the first time ever, American confirms Starlink is coming to literally every mainline plane, and United's weird new economy seat finally gets a price tag.
Hey, welcome to Runway Briefing for Friday, October 9th. Let's get into it.

So, remember yesterday we talked about that Houthi missile hitting a parked Saudia jet at Riyadh airport? Update, and it's not a good one.

Three people are now confirmed dead, including a Saudia captain, and there were two separate missile attacks that day, not one. A dozen airlines have pulled their Riyadh flights through the weekend — Lufthansa, Etihad, Qatar, you name it.

And right in the middle of this, Delta is supposed to launch Atlanta to Riyadh in two weeks. Ed Bastian went on CNBC and basically said the quiet part out loud — "safety is going to make the call." Which is corporate-speak for "we might pull this thing."

Here's what makes it worse though. Delta is reportedly telling its own employees not to travel to Riyadh right now, while still planning to fly paying passengers there October 23rd. That's a tough needle to thread.

This route was already controversial — it's tied to Delta's partnership with Riyadh Air, the Saudi-backed startup, and some had doubts about the timing before any of this happened. Now you've got a warzone-adjacent airport and a CEO who sounds like he's already writing the cancellation announcement. I'd be shocked if this launches on schedule.

Okay, happier plane news. Southwest might actually buy widebodies. I know, let that sink in.

Multiple outlets are reporting Southwest is "strongly considering" Boeing 787s — dozens of them, new or used — to finally push into long-haul international flying. This is a company that has flown nothing but 737s for its entire fifty-plus year history. That's the whole identity.

CEO Bob Jordan has talked about eight to twelve long-haul destinations, Europe and Latin America probably first. But here's my question — do they actually need a whole new aircraft type? Some analysts are pointing out that an A321XLR or even a tricked-out MAX could get you to parts of Europe without Southwest having to build an entirely new maintenance and training program from scratch. Widebodies are expensive to operate and expensive to staff. This is a huge strategic bet for an airline that's spent decades being the simple, one-plane-type, no-frills guys. Worth watching closely.

Next — American just went all in on Starlink. We talked about this a week or two back when the rumors started, but now it's official. Every single mainline jet, more than a thousand aircraft, getting Starlink.

That's a big jump from their original plan, which was only about half the fleet. So why the change? Simple — Delta and United are racing each other to slap Starlink on planes as fast as possible, and American doesn't want to be the airline with spotty wifi while everyone else brags about streaming Netflix at 35,000 feet. This is pure competitive pressure, and honestly, passengers win.

And finally, United's new Relax Row. You've probably seen renderings of this — it's this weird in-between seat, not quite premium economy, not quite regular coach, debuting in 2027 on the 787s and 777s. Up to twelve of these sections per plane, rolling out to over 200 widebodies by 2030.

What's smart about this is it doesn't require United to rip out a whole cabin and dedicate real estate to a new class. They're just upselling existing economy seats for a bit more recline and space. It's basically "economy plus, but we gave it a spa name." Expect the price to land somewhere between basic economy and premium economy — enough to make you think about it, not enough to make it a real upgrade. Classic middle-seat psychology, just dressed up nicer.

Quick one before we go — a United flight attendant accused a 70-year-old passenger of sexual assault because the guy's hand brushed his while putting trash in a bag. The man got pulled off the plane and questioned by police in South Korea. I don't even have a joke here, it's just a mess, and I feel for that family.
