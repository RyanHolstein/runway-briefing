Episode title: Fuel Prices Bite, JetBlue Digs In, and the A380 Keeps Getting Quietly Retired
Episode Summary: Jet fuel is spiking again and WestJet just became the latest airline to feel it, slashing fifteen international routes overnight. Plus, JetBlue and Frontier both posted ugly headline losses but tell very different stories underneath, the A380 keeps losing real estate at two more airlines, and Heathrow just blew up a twelve-year partnership between Delta and Virgin Atlantic.
Hey, welcome to Runway Briefing for Wednesday, October 28th. Let's get into it.

So, fuel. The Air Current had a piece out saying the whole industry is creeping toward a correction, and here's why that's not just analyst talk. Jet fuel prices have been climbing all year thanks to the Iran situation, and a strong dollar is cushioning US carriers while basically everyone else eats it raw.

And we've already got a real casualty. WestJet just cancelled service to fifteen destinations, gone, not reduced, cancelled. They're blaming soft transborder demand and fuel volatility tied directly to the Iran conflict. This is a Calgary-based airline pulling back its international map in real time because the math stopped working. When The Air Current says "verge of correction," this is what that actually looks like on the ground.

Speaking of airlines feeling the squeeze, we got two earnings reports this week that look bad on the surface but mean very different things. JetBlue posted a 257 million dollar quarterly loss and their stock hit an all-time low on Monday. Remember when we talked about United maybe circling JetBlue as a buyout target? CEO Joanna Geraghty shut that down hard this week, said they're not in restructuring talks with creditors, full stop, still doubling down on premium seats under the JetForward plan.

Frontier's numbers tell the opposite story. Record revenue, but a 104 million dollar loss, which sounds terrible until you realize 134 million of that was a one-time charge for returning leased planes early. Strip that out and they actually made 40 million bucks. For an ultra-low-cost carrier, that's genuinely encouraging, because the whole bare-bones model has been looking shaky industry-wide.

Okay, the A380. Two stories today, same theme. Qatar Airways just pulled every single A380 flight off one of its major long-haul routes, and British Airways cut its A380 Heathrow routes down to just four, out of twelve jets they actually own. These planes cost airlines something like 400 million dollars each to buy, and they're thirsty four-engine beasts in a world where twin-engine jets do the same job for way less fuel. Nobody's building new A380s. Every year there are fewer places left flying them, and it's basically just a countdown now.

Heathrow news, and this one's petty in a fun way. The airport is splitting up Delta and Virgin Atlantic, who've shared Terminal 3 for twelve years under their transatlantic joint venture. Starting in 2028, Delta gets moved out, more than a mile away, which means Delta's business class flyers lose access to the Virgin Clubhouse. Virgin is reportedly furious, and honestly, fair, because this partnership only works well when everyone's in the same building coordinating connections and lounges together.

Quick fun one to close. Alaska Airlines announced nonstop flights from Anchorage to Tokyo starting next May, which sounds great until you hear it's on a 737 MAX 8. Eight hours and fifteen minutes, domestic recliner seats, no seatback screens, bring your own iPad. It's a clever route for Alaskans who'd otherwise connect through Seattle, I just wouldn't want to be in seat 34B for that one.

And last thing, because I can't not mention it. A Delta customer posted the "warm, empathetic" apology email customer service sent them, and somebody forgot to delete the AI prompt notes at the top of the message. So the apology that was supposed to feel warm and human literally announced it was designed to feel warm and human. You cannot make this up.
