Episode Title: American's Widebody Gamble, Boeing's Air Force One Cracks, and Ukraine Warns the Skies Over Russia Are Closing

Episode Summary: American Airlines is dangling up to 65 widebody jets in front of Airbus after years of retiring planes it probably shouldn't have. Boeing's Air Force One replacement is now getting patched for cracks on top of a $3 billion overrun, and Zelensky just told every airline still flying over Russia that the sky is about to get a lot less friendly. Plus, Emirates finally makes premium economy look like a real cabin instead of an afterthought.

Hey, welcome to Runway Briefing for Friday, September 4th. Let's get into it.

So American Airlines is shopping for widebodies again, and this one actually matters. They're replacing 47 aging 777-200ERs, and the order could go as high as 65 jets. Boeing's the obvious pick because of fleet commonality, but American's Chief Commercial Officer basically said out loud that Airbus is very much back in the running.

Here's the thing though — this doesn't fix American's real problem. They spent the last few years retiring widebodies faster than they replaced them, and deliveries on a new order wouldn't land until the 2030s. So even if they pick a winner today, American's basically admitting they gave away international growth opportunities they can't get back anytime soon. Delta and United have been eating their lunch on long-haul for a while now, and this order, whichever way it goes, is American playing catch-up, not playing offense.

Now, Boeing. Remember the Air Force One saga? It just keeps getting worse. Bloomberg's reporting Boeing is now fixing hundreds of microscopic cracks in structural stringers on the two 747-8s being converted for the next presidential fleet. This is on top of three billion dollars — with a B — in cost overruns Boeing's already eating because of a fixed-price contract they never should have signed. These planes were supposed to be delivered years ago. At this point I think the safest prediction in aviation is that Air Force One arrives whenever Boeing finally stops finding new things wrong with it.

Okay, this next one is genuinely unsettling. Zelensky put out a direct warning this week to airlines, insurers, and basically anyone still flying over Russia — he says Russian airspace is "becoming completely unsafe" because Ukraine is ramping up long-range drone strikes deep inside the country. And to be clear, Ukraine says it's not targeting civilian planes. That's not really the point. The point is you just need enough drone activity near Moscow and St. Petersburg that airports keep shutting down and insurers start refusing to cover the risk. That's basically how you close an airspace without ever touching an airliner. Chinese, Turkish, and Gulf carriers are still flying through, but if this keeps escalating, don't be shocked if that list gets shorter fast.

On a lighter note — Emirates finally nailed premium economy. They just unveiled the next-gen seat going on new A350s, and it's the first fully electric premium economy seat in the industry. Wireless charging, 39 inches of pitch, only 28 seats in the whole cabin. Genuinely impressive. What's funny is Tim Clark almost didn't launch this thing at all — he was worried it'd cannibalize business class. Instead it turned into one of Emirates' biggest moneymakers, which just tells you Qatar Airways is being stubborn for no reason. Akbar Al Baker used to insist Qatar's economy seat was basically premium economy already. It wasn't. It still isn't. And with Al Baker long gone, there's genuinely no excuse left for Qatar not to build one of these.

Quick one to close — American had a scare at LaGuardia yesterday. A 737 aborted takeoff at 140 miles an hour because the crew thought they hit a bird. Turned out to be nothing, no damage found. But that's exactly the system working the way it's supposed to — high-speed abort, no hesitation, everyone's fine. Good reminder that boring outcomes are the goal in this business.
