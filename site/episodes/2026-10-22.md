Episode title: United's Texas-Sized Lounge, American's Premium Gamble, and Avelo Hits Reverse
Episode Summary: United opens the largest airline lounge in America and it sounds genuinely wild — barista coffee and all. Plus, American's losing money even as premium revenue booms, Avelo quietly pulls out of nine cities, and a guy's life jacket prank gets him banned from two airlines.

Hey, welcome to Runway Briefing for Thursday, October 22nd. Let's get into it.

So United opened a lounge in Houston today and I need you to understand the scale here. Fifty-four thousand square feet. Eight hundred seats. A outdoor terrace. Two bars. Eleven private phone rooms. This is, officially, the largest airline lounge in the Americas.

People who got early tours are comparing it to a Gulf carrier lounge — like Emirates or Qatar level — which, if you know United's SVP of network planning was literally in the room nodding along to that comparison, tells you they're not shy about it. There's barista coffee, which only exists in one other United Club in the entire system. There's brisket. There's a taqueria. It's basically a food hall with jet bridges attached.

Here's why this matters beyond "ooh, shiny." Lounges used to be an afterthought — a room with stale pretzels and bad wifi. Now they're a competitive weapon. Airlines know premium travelers will pick a connection based on the lounge alone, and United's basically saying "come connect through Houston, we built you a palace." It's part of a bigger arms race — Delta's doing it, American's doing it — everybody's realized the real money is in making rich people comfortable, not in cramming more economy seats in.

Which, speaking of premium bets — American reported earnings today, and it's a weird one. Revenue's up 19%. But losses actually got worse. While Delta, United, and Southwest all made money this quarter, American didn't.

CEO Robert Isom's spin is "premium performance is leading the way," and honestly, digging into the numbers, he's not totally wrong — their premium cabins are doing well. The problem is everything else. Basic economy, the main cabin, isn't covering its costs relative to everybody else. So American's stuck in this spot where their best product is working but their biggest product, the one that fills most of the plane, is dragging the whole thing down. That's not a great place to be when your competitors are all profitable and you're explaining why you're still not.

Okay, lightning round. Avelo Airlines is quietly pulling out of nine airports this year — including dropping Houston Hobby entirely, and leaving Jacksonville and Knoxville in November. If you've been half paying attention to Avelo, this tracks. They've been restructuring their whole route map for months now, chasing whatever's profitable and abandoning what isn't. It's the ultra-low-cost playbook right now — grow fast, realize half of it doesn't work, retreat. We've seen this movie with Spirit, we've seen it with Wizz Air's cuts just this week. The ultra-low-cost model is having a rough year across the board.

And finally — a guy inflated his life jacket during the safety demo on a Jetstar flight from Sydney to Adelaide. For a video. His brother, sitting right next to him, literally covered his face in the footage. The payoff? Police met him at the gate, and he's now banned from both Jetstar and Qantas.

Look, I get it, safety demos are boring. But pulling the inflation cord on actual emergency equipment isn't a bit, it's the kind of thing that can delay a flight and mess with everyone else's day. He says he's learned his lesson. Sure. Expensive lesson for a few seconds of content, though.
