Episode title — NTSB Fires Back at the Times, a Pilot's Cocaine Problem, and BA Hits the Runway Twice
Episode Summary: The NTSB goes to war with the New York Times over a safety story it says is political hit job dressed up as journalism. Plus, an American Airlines pilot is in a UK jail cell after allegedly trying to fly high on cocaine, a British Airways A321neo smacks its tail on the runway not once but twice on live TV, and KLM decides the way to save money is to fly smaller planes — which is the opposite of what everyone else is doing.

Hey, welcome to Runway Briefing for Friday, November 13th. Let's get into it.

So the NTSB is picking a fight with the New York Times, and it's getting spicy. The Times has a big Sunday magazine piece coming out called "Is Air Travel Getting Less Safe?" — written by James Fallows, using the DCA midair collision from back in January as the entry point. The NTSB's official account went on X and basically torched it, saying the article "distorts" chair Jennifer Homendy's comments in a "clear attempt to malign the President and his Administration."

Here's the thing — the NTSB is supposed to be the boring, apolitical safety agency. They investigate crashes, they issue reports, they don't usually do public media takedowns on social media. So when they come out swinging like this, that itself is kind of a story. The article apparently frames what's happening to aviation safety under the current administration as part of a broader pattern — alongside public health, higher ed, diplomacy, you name it. Whether that's a fair read or a reach, I don't know, I haven't seen the full piece yet. But watching the actual accident investigators publicly accuse a major newspaper of political spin — that's not nothing. This one's going to run for a few news cycles.

Okay, next. An American Airlines pilot is sitting in a British jail right now, and it is not a good look. Wayne Sigler, 64, was arrested at Heathrow before he was supposed to fly to Chicago, and he's pleaded guilty to cocaine possession. He's also facing a separate charge of being unfit to fly due to drink or drugs, which, you know, is the charge you really don't want stacked on top of the first one.

Two flights got canceled that day because of this. And UK courts do not mess around with this stuff — remember the Delta captain a couple years back with the half-empty bottle of Jägermeister in his carry-on? Ten months in prison. Sigler's due in Crown Court next month, which has way more sentencing power than where he's been so far. Not a great week to be in American's pilot HR department.

Speaking of things going wrong on landing — a British Airways A321neo tail-struck the runway at Heathrow. Twice. In one landing attempt. It was all caught live on Big Jet TV, so this is now a very viral, very embarrassing video for BA. The plane bounced, went around, came back and landed fine the second time, everyone deplaned normal. But investigators from the AAIB are now involved, and the jet's grounded for inspections.

Weather was calm, clear skies, nothing dramatic going on — which honestly makes this weirder. A double tail strike on a routine approach in good conditions is the kind of thing that makes you go, what exactly happened up there? We'll see what the investigation turns up.

Alright, strategy story. KLM just posted results, and on paper it looks fine — 11.4 percent operating margin for the quarter. But zoom out to the first nine months of the year and that number drops to under 5 percent. So the CEO's out there talking about "far-reaching, sometimes painful measures," and the actual plan is interesting: KLM wants to shrink its planes.

That's the opposite of what basically every other airline is doing right now. Everyone's chasing bigger gauge, more seats per flight, better unit economics through scale. KLM's betting that smaller aircraft, flown more efficiently, gets them to profitability faster than brute-force size. It's a contrarian move in an industry that loves following the leader, and I'll be curious whether this actually works or whether KLM just quietly grows the planes back in two years once the spreadsheet stops looking scary.

Last one — Alaska Airlines is adding five new routes out of Seattle for 2027: Charlotte, Norfolk, Jacksonville, Des Moines year-round, plus a seasonal run to Guadalajara. What's notable here is the timing — this comes right after Alaska posted a rough third quarter with profits falling sharply. So they're expanding Seattle while the balance sheet is wobbling, which tells you how much they're betting on SEA as their fortress hub. Big swing while things are shaky. We'll see if it pays off.
