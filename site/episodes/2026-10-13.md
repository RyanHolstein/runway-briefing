Episode Title: United Makes Status Harder to Get, a Fake Hijack Shuts Down Nuremberg, and Emirates Might Ditch the 787
Episode Summary: United overhauls MileagePlus and frequent flyers are not happy, a wrong squawk code sends fighter jets scrambling over Germany, and Emirates is reportedly ready to swap its entire Dreamliner order for more 777Xs. Plus, an update on the Flydubai investigation, Burbank's brand new terminal opens, and Qantas loses a 787 to bedbugs.
Hey, welcome to Runway Briefing for Tuesday, October 13th. Let's get into it.

So, United. Big loyalty program shakeup today, and if you're a 1K flyer, you might want to sit down.

Starting in 2027, United is killing off the flights-plus-points path to status. From now on, it's PQPs only — meaning dollars, basically. Doesn't matter if you fly sixty segments a year, all that matters is what you spent.

And the top tier, 1K, is getting way harder to hit — 32,000 Premier Qualifying Points, which for someone qualifying on the old flights-and-points combo is a 45% jump. United's calling this "simplifying." Which, sure, one path is simpler than two. But simpler and harder aren't mutually exclusive, and this is both.

Here's the part that bugs me: United's betting that their most loyal flyers are too locked in to leave. Meanwhile Alaska's Atmos program is literally doing the opposite right now, adding more ways to qualify, not fewer. So while United's squeezing the top of the pyramid, Alaska's rolling out the welcome mat. One of these is going to look smart in a year. I don't think it's United.

Quick update on something we've been following. Remember Israel's new rule banning aircrew with dual nationality from 31 countries? Turns out it's bigger than we thought — it also applies to flights just overflying Israeli airspace, not only ones landing there. That's a real headache for carriers routing through the eastern Mediterranean, because now you've got to check every crew member's passport history before you can even fly over the country, let alone land in it.

And on the Flydubai attack itself — two more Maldivian co-pilots are now in UAE custody, reportedly colleagues of the attacker who lived in the same building. UAE investigators have said publicly the attacker acted alone. These detentions suggest someone wants to be very, very sure about that before closing the file.

Weirder story now. A Corendon Airlines 737 flying into Nuremberg this morning squawked 7500 — that's the hijack code — and the airport basically stopped. Fighter jets scrambled, operations shut down, the whole drill. Turned out to be a mistaken code entry, nobody onboard had any idea anything was wrong. Which, I get it, better safe than sorry, but imagine being a passenger on that flight and finding out after landing that you'd apparently been hijacked without noticing.

Emirates next, and this one's a fleet story worth watching. They're reportedly considering converting their entire remaining Boeing 787 order — about 30 jets — into more 777Xs instead. Emirates is already sitting on hundreds of 777X orders, and Boeing's been slow getting that program certified, so you'd think Emirates would want the diversification the 787 offers. Instead it sounds like they're doubling down on one airplane type for their whole widebody strategy. That's a lot of eggs in a Boeing basket that still hasn't delivered a single 777X to anyone.

Good news out of Burbank, for once. The new $1.3 billion terminal at Hollywood Burbank Airport opened to the public today, replacing a nearly 100-year-old building that was, frankly, a safety hazard — it sat way too close to the runway, and a Southwest 737 actually ran off the end of it back in 2000. Took about 40 years of fighting NIMBY opposition to get this built, and Cranky Flier was out there for the preview — says it's a real upgrade without losing the airport's no-jet-bridge charm. Jay Leno showed up and asked everyone to keep it a secret. Good luck with that, Jay.

And finally, Qantas had to cancel a Rome-to-Perth flight over the weekend because their 787 got infested with bedbugs. Not a mechanical issue, not weather — bedbugs. The plane's getting treated, passengers got rebooked, and somewhere a very unlucky maintenance crew is having the worst day in the hangar.
