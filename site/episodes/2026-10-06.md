So, Boeing. Again. Southwest and United have both told Boeing they will not accept new 737 MAX deliveries if they're running a specific flight-management-computer software that's been flagged as an issue. CNBC broke this one.

Think about that for a second. These are two of Boeing's biggest customers, and they're literally sending planes back before they even leave the factory. That's not a great vibe for a company that's already been living under a microscope since the door plug blew off an Alaska jet.

We don't know yet exactly what the software does wrong, but when your two biggest 737 customers both say "nope" on the same day, that's not a coincidence, that's a pattern. Boeing's whole recovery story has been "we're fixing quality control." Every one of these stories undoes a little bit of that narrative. I'll be watching to see if this delays deliveries industry-wide, because Southwest and United aren't exactly known for being picky.

Okay, next. United is going hard at Starlink. They're installing it on more than a thousand mainline jets by May of 2027, and the pace is wild, over twenty installations a week across seventeen maintenance stations.

Here's why this matters. Delta's Wi-Fi deal is with Amazon, not Starlink, and that rollout doesn't even start until 2028 at the earliest, with just 500 planes in the first wave. So United isn't just upgrading Wi-Fi, they're trying to win the "which airline doesn't have garbage internet" race before Delta even shows up to compete. Remember yesterday when we talked about United taking shots at Delta over Wi-Fi? This is the receipts. They're not just talking trash, they're actually out-building them.

Now, British Airways. They unveiled the full retrofit for their A380s, and the headline number is wild: 106 business class seats, making it the biggest business class cabin in the world. The entire upper deck, all of it, is becoming Club World. Eighty-eight economy seats, gone.

This is a big bet on an airplane most of the industry has already given up on. Qantas retired theirs early, remember we talked about that math, four engines just don't pencil out against a 787 anymore. But BA has twelve of these things and no plans to retire them, so instead of fighting the economics, they're just going all in on luxury. New lounge at Heathrow too, with 59 showers and private cabanas, because apparently regular showers weren't cutting it anymore.

Honestly, if you're flying coach on one of these, you're basically carry-on luggage for the business class experience happening upstairs.

Quick one that's actually kind of funny. United just announced new flights to Flagstaff, Arizona, twice daily from Denver starting next summer. Less than 48 hours after Delta announced its own new Flagstaff service from Salt Lake City. American's been flying there for decades from Phoenix and Dallas and had the place to themselves.

Flagstaff got an $800,000 federal grant to attract new service, and all of a sudden every airline in the country remembered that the Grand Canyon and Sedona exist. Small town, tiny regional jets, sudden main event. I love it when three airlines all have the same idea in the same week and nobody wants to say they copied each other.
