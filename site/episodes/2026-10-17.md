So, the 737 MAX 10. Boeing and the FAA had their final certification meeting Friday, and by all accounts, it went well. This plane could be certified as soon as next week.

Let's just sit with that for a second. The MAX 10 was supposed to enter service in 2020. Six years late. Six years of two crashes, a grounding, whistleblowers, a door plug blowing off a different MAX, and congressional hearings. And now, finally, maybe, it's close.

But here's the twist — some airlines reportedly might not even want the jets right away. There's a software glitch that's got operators nervous, and nobody wants to be the launch customer for a brand-new problem on a plane that's already been cursed six ways from Sunday. United and Delta have hundreds of these on order. So does this unlock a flood of deliveries, or does Boeing certify a plane that airlines quietly slow-walk accepting? I think we find out fast, because Boeing desperately needs the cash flow from MAX 10 deliveries. This one's not over just because the FAA signs off.

Okay, next. Trump wants nonstop flights between the US and Lebanon again, first time since 1985. That ban happened after the TWA Flight 847 hijacking — the one where Navy diver Robert Stethem was murdered. So restoring this route is a genuinely big diplomatic moment.

Except — and this is the part Trump's order can't fix — Beirut's airport just failed an ICAO safety review. No valid certification, no real emergency plan, firefighting equipment that can't handle widebody jets. Lebanon says they're fixing it, certification work started yesterday. But US airline officials are telling Reuters, quietly, nobody's rushing back. You can lift a political ban with a signature. You cannot sign away a firetruck shortage. The FAA and TSA still have to actually sign off, and that's a separate, much slower process.

Switching gears — some actual good travel news. The US just lifted restrictions on Mexican airlines flying new routes out of Mexico City. Remember, about a year ago, the DOT blocked thirteen routes from Aeromexico, Volaris, and Viva Aerobus over a dispute about cargo slots at Mexico City's airport. That fight's over now, as of Friday. Mexican carriers can go apply for new US routes again. This matters because Mexico is one of the fastest-growing international markets for US travel, and a year of frozen expansion is a year US and Mexican travelers didn't get new nonstop options. Good to see it resolved.

Quick one — an American Airlines 737 flying out of O'Hare yesterday had to declare a mayday and turn around after smoke started pouring out of a galley trash can. Pilot literally radioed "fire in the cabin." Everyone landed safely, no injuries, 180 people on board. American scrambled a replacement plane, got folks to Dallas about four hours late. Not a huge story, but a good reminder — lithium battery fires in trash cans are becoming a real recurring issue industry-wide, and this is exactly why flight attendants are trained to treat any smoke like it's the real thing immediately.

And finally, British Airways is cracking down on people secretly filming crew and other passengers with Meta smart glasses. You can still wear them, but if BA catches you using the camera to covertly record someone without consent, they can kick you off the flight — and refuse your return journey too. Your whole round-trip, gone, over a sneaky video. Honestly, good. Nobody wants to find out they went viral because some guy's glasses were quietly rolling the entire flight.
