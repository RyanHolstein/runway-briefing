Episode Title: Lufthansa Snags TAP, Delta Gets Fancy, and Riyadh Air Flees Its Own Airport
Episode Summary: Lufthansa looks set to win a key stake in TAP Air Portugal, beating out Air France-KLM in a fight that's been brewing for years. Plus, Delta rolls out $200 bottles of wine in Delta One, Riyadh Air evacuates its brand-new Dreamliners as missile attacks continue, and Airbus drops some numbers on just how many workers this industry is going to need.

Hey, welcome to Runway Briefing for Wednesday, October 14th. Let's get into it.

So, Lufthansa versus Air France-KLM. This has been a slow-motion fight over Portugal's flag carrier, and it looks like Lufthansa just won.

Portuguese broadcaster SIC is reporting Lufthansa is the preferred bidder for a 44.9% stake in TAP Air Portugal. Air France-KLM offered cash plus shares. Lufthansa just offered cash, and apparently their balance sheet made the government feel a lot more comfortable.

Here's why this matters. TAP gives whoever buys it a Lisbon hub, which is basically the best gateway in Europe for flights to Brazil and the rest of Portuguese-speaking Africa. Lufthansa already owns pieces of Austrian, Swiss, Brussels Airlines, ITA in Italy. This would be hub number five.

Air France-KLM loses a chance to lock down their own southern European foothold, and honestly, you have to wonder how many more of these consolidation plays are even left on the board in Europe. The independent national carrier is basically an endangered species at this point. No official decision yet, Portugal's cabinet was supposed to meet on this, so we'll see if it's actually finalized soon.

Okay, Riyadh. We've been following this all week, so quick update. Riyadh Air, the shiny new Saudi startup airline, had to evacuate three of its brand-new 787 Dreamliners out of King Khalid Airport on Tuesday. Twenty-five minutes between each departure. They scattered them to AlUla and Medina.

Remember, this is an airline that hasn't even really started normal operations yet. They've got routes announced to like fifteen cities and basically none of them are flying because the airport keeps getting hit by missiles. The airport's shut until at least October 17th, and that's the optimistic version. When your airline's biggest operational achievement so far is successfully fleeing its own hub, that's not a great look, through no fault of their own, to be fair.

Delta. They've been teasing this new Delta One experience for months, and today they finally showed their hand. Hong Kong-style sea bass, Wagyu burgers, two-hundred-dollar bottles of Silver Oak Cabernet, Blanton's bourbon, Champagne poured over sorbet. This is not airplane food anymore, this is a restaurant with wings.

It starts November 2nd out of LA, on routes like Auckland, and rolls out network-wide by early 2027. And this connects to the new Delta One Suite too, the reverse herringbone seat debuting on the A350-1000 next year, with forty-four inches of pitch, which beats what American and United are putting on their newest planes.

Here's the thing though. Delta's betting that the way you win premium customers now is by matching what they get in the lounge with what they get in the air. Which, fair, if you're paying Delta One prices, you probably don't want a downgrade in food quality once the door closes.

Quick one from Airbus. Their commercial aircraft CEO said in Toulouse that a decision on stretching the A220 and the A350 is coming before 2030. Both planes have had stretch rumors swirling all year, and he basically confirmed yeah, airlines want it.

Why do airlines want bigger versions of planes that already exist? Because a stretched A220 eats into Boeing's 737 territory, and a stretched A350 could threaten the 777X before it's even really out the door. Airbus isn't promising anything yet, but when a CEO says "clearly there's demand," that's corporate speak for "we're doing this, just give us the paperwork time."

And last one, kind of a fun detail. Air Tahiti just took delivery of a new ATR 72, but this one's wild: it's all business class. Twenty-six seats, one-one seating, for inter-island hops in French Polynesia that last under an hour. It's basically a flying first-class lounge for people going to Bora Bora who have more money than flight time.

Getting it there, though, is its own story. Eight flights, six days, Toulouse to Cairo to Muscat to Hyderabad, all the way across the globe to Tahiti. A regional turboprop crossing basically half the planet to spend its life flying hour-long hops. Kind of poetic, honestly.
