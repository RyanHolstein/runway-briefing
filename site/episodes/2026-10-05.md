Episode Title: British Airways' Scary Descent, American Kills "Invols," and the Flydubai Plot Gets Darker
Episode Summary: A British Airways 777 loses pressurization over the Irish Sea and drops 27,000 feet in under ten minutes — we break down why the real drama happened after the masks came down. Plus, American Airlines says no more bumping passengers off oversold flights, new details emerge on just how long the flydubai attacker was planning his attack, and American can't decide if it's adding routes or cutting them.

Hey, welcome to Runway Briefing for Monday, October 5th. Let's get into it.

So, British Airways flight 295, London to Chicago, this afternoon. Boeing 777, thirty years old, climbing through 36,000 feet over the Irish Sea when the plane just... stops pressurizing. Masks drop, pilots throw it into an emergency descent, and they lose 27,000 feet of altitude in under ten minutes. That's the scary part. Here's the part that's actually more interesting: they couldn't just turn around and land.

The plane was too heavy. So for over an hour, this 777 is flying a holding pattern off the coast of southwest Britain, at 9,000 feet, landing gear down just to create drag, basically burning off fuel so it's light enough to land safely. Everybody's fine, nobody needed oxygen once they got down to breathable altitude. But think about that for a second — you survive the scary emergency descent, and then you get to spend an hour circling the ocean waiting for the plane to get skinny enough to land. Aviation is wild.

Okay, update on flydubai, because this story keeps getting worse. Remember the co-pilot who attacked his captain with a crash axe trying to crash the plane into Tel Aviv's airport? New reporting says he'd planned this before he even got hired at flydubai — he specifically went looking for airlines that flew to Israel. And get this, he'd already tried once before, on an earlier flight to Tel Aviv, and lost his nerve.

Oman Air had grounded this guy years ago over extremist views and never charged him with anything, so when flydubai came calling, there was nothing on paper to stop them. Now Netanyahu's ordered a full security review of every foreign airline flying into Israel. Which sounds reasonable until you realize where that logic goes — foreign crews getting background-checked by a country they don't even work for. Nobody's figured out how that's supposed to work yet.

Alright, lighter news. American Airlines says it's done with involuntary bumping. As of September 30th, the new rule is simple: keep the door open, keep raising the offer, until somebody volunteers. No more dragging a random family off the plane because the flight sold too well. Gate agents can now approve compensation above whatever the old cap was, and volunteers can even get rebooked onto flights that show as sold out.

Honestly, good for them. This is the airline that, what, ten years ago, had a guy dragged off a United flight — different airline, I know, but that moment basically forced this entire industry to rethink bumping. The real test now is whether American actually backs up its gate agents when this gets expensive, or whether "no invols" quietly turns into "mostly no invols" by January.

And American, same day, also announced it's adding a new nonstop, JFK to Seattle, seven hours each way. Which sounds great until you remember they also just axed five routes to, quote, "strengthen the airline." That's corporate speak for "these weren't making money." So American's simultaneously growing and shrinking its map this week, which tells you where we are in the industry right now — everybody's still trying to find the routes that actually pencil out, instead of just flying everywhere because they can.

Quick one to close. U.S. airline fuel costs just hit six point one billion dollars — and get this, fuel consumption is actually down four and a half percent. So airlines are burning less gas and still paying more for it. That's the whole story of 2026 in one stat, and it's exactly why your holiday ticket keeps creeping up every time you check.
