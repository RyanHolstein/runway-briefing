Episode title: Delta Declares War on Heathrow, American's Ebola Paperwork Problem, and Southwest Admits the Quiet Part Out Loud
Episode Summary: Delta's CEO is threatening to sue Heathrow Airport over a terminal shuffle that breaks up their cozy setup with Virgin Atlantic — and he's not being quiet about it. Plus, American flight attendants get a new Ebola attestation requirement with a scary catch, Southwest finally admits what "low-cost loyalty" actually meant, and a tennis star's bags make it to the gate without her.
Hey, welcome to Runway Briefing for Tuesday, November 3rd. Let's get into it.

So, Delta is mad. Like, lawyer-up mad.

Heathrow just did its once-a-decade terminal review — basically deciding who sits where for the next ten years — and Delta got bounced from Terminal 3 to Terminal 4. Doesn't sound like much, right? Except Terminal 4 is a mile and a half away, bus ride required, and Delta shares Terminal 3 with Virgin Atlantic, which they own 49% of and run a joint venture with.

Delta spent years and real money integrating with Virgin in that terminal — shared check-in, shared lounges, synced connections, especially onward to India. Split them up, and now a connecting passenger has to bus across the airfield. CEO Ed Bastian called the move "short-sighted" and is now openly threatening legal action.

Here's why this matters beyond one airline's feelings: Heathrow also bumped Finnair, JAL, China Southern, and LATAM. This is Heathrow flexing its power as basically the only slot-constrained mega-hub in Europe, and airlines have very little leverage when the airport just says "move." Delta's betting that threatening a lawsuit changes that math. We'll see if it actually does.

Next — if you're an American Airlines flight attendant, pay attention to this one, because it kicks in tonight. Starting at midnight tonight, anyone flying into the US internationally has to sign an Ebola attestation, confirming they haven't been in the Democratic Republic of Congo in the last 21 days. That's a CDC order, not an airline thing.

Here's the part that's wild — flight attendants have to verbally attest this to the captain, every single international arrival, and if they forget or refuse? They're stuck in a foreign country, on their own dime. Airlines actually asked the CDC to exempt crew, since they already log health stuff on the standard GenDec form. CDC said no. So now there's a real chance someone gets stranded in Frankfurt because of a paperwork technicality. Rough way to find out your form wasn't filed.

Okay, Southwest. New numbers out, and they basically confirm what we all suspected — sixty percent of Southwest flyers are now paying extra to upgrade seats. Sixty percent.

This connects to that bigger story going around about airline status this week — United's making 2027 elite status about how much you spend, not how much you fly, and Southwest's doing the same thing with its Companion Pass, tying it to credit card spend instead of just flights. Basically, "loyalty" used to mean showing up. Now it means swiping. Southwest built its whole brand on no change fees and free bags, and bit by bit, they've walked all of it back. The upgrade number just proves people will pay — so why wouldn't they keep charging?

Quick one that's actually kind of cool — Alaska Airlines is launching daily flights from Anchorage to Tokyo next summer. And no, this isn't about connecting Alaska to the lower 48 — the flight times don't even line up for that.

Cranky Flier had a great read on this: Alaska's trying to turn Anchorage into Honolulu. Japanese travelers fly to Hawaii for vacation all the time — Alaska's betting some of them will do the same thing for Alaska instead. It's a small market, a weird bet, and honestly kind of ballsy for an airline that usually plays it safe.

And last one, because it's just funny — tennis star Jessica Pegula says her United flight got canceled, then un-canceled, then took off without her. Her bags made it. She did not.

If you've ever stood at a gate watching your suitcase leave without you, you know the specific rage she's feeling right now.
