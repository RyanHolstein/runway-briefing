Runway Briefing – United Goes Wild, American Plays It Cool
Episode Summary: United drops ten new international routes at a big Newark event while American quietly adds three new cities from Philly. A tale of two airline strategies.

Hey, welcome to Runway Briefing for Friday, August 28th. Let's get into it.

So it's that time of year — summer route announcement season — and United went absolutely nuts.

They held this big fancy event in Newark, because apparently you need a stage and lighting to announce flights now, and dropped ten new international destinations. Eight of them aren't served by any other U.S. airline. Eight of the ten are flying out of Newark, which, sure, someone's gotta get people out of Jersey.

We're talking Luxembourg, Ljubljana, Olbia, Catania, Ibiza, Valencia, Marseille, Terceira. That's basically a Mediterranean bucket list, and United's putting a Newark gate on all of it. Most of these are seasonal, most start next spring, and a lot are flying on the A321XLR, which is quickly becoming the plane that makes weird little routes actually pencil out.

San Francisco gets Okinawa, which is a first for any U.S. carrier — nobody's flown there nonstop before. Dulles gets Toulouse. And on top of the new cities, United's adding routes to places already on the map — Denver to Paris, LA to Osaka, San Francisco to Tel Aviv is coming back, and Dulles to Milan.

Here's the fun detail, courtesy of Cranky Flier — two of those "exclusive" cities, Olbia and Catania, actually just got JFK service too. Delta ran one of those fan-vote things where people pick the next destination, and Olbia and Catania won. So really this was Sardinia and Sicily's week, not just United's.

Point is, United's not messing around. Ten new spots, mostly Europe and Asia, mostly out of one airport. Newark's either becoming the best gateway to Europe in the country or the most crowded TSA line in America. Possibly both.

Now, American saw all this and said, hold on, we do announcements too.

They rolled out seven new international routes of their own this week, though only three of them are actually brand new cities — the rest are... let's call them reruns. The new ones are Porto, Reykjavik, and Vienna, all flying out of Philadelphia, which is American's version of Newark I guess — the hub they keep throwing new stuff at.

It's a smaller swing than United's, and honestly it kind of has to be, since American's playing catch-up on transatlantic route count compared to Delta and United lately. But Porto and Reykjavik are solid adds — Iceland traffic keeps climbing every year, and Porto's been an underserved gap for a while now.

So look, big picture: United had the flashier week, no doubt. Newark event, ten cities, exclusive routes, the whole production. American had the quieter, more targeted week — three new spots, playing to their Philly strength. Both are betting big on international demand holding up into next summer, which, fingers crossed for all of us, because these fares aren't getting any cheaper if that demand doesn't show up.
