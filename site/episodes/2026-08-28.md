Runway Briefing — Episode: United Goes Big, American Follows, and a Quiet Reminder Fees Never Sleep
Episode Summary: United drops a massive international route announcement out of Newark, American answers with its own — smaller but sharper — expansion, and we close with a quick note on why route wars are really just airlines fighting over your layover habits.

Hey, welcome to Runway Briefing for Friday, August 28th. Let's get into it.

So it's route announcement season, and United decided to just... go big. Like, "rent out a venue in Newark and invite everybody" big.

Ten new international destinations. Eight of them aren't served by any US airline right now. Not "we're adding a second daily flight" — actual new dots on the map. And get this — eight of the ten are flying out of Newark, which, fine, United basically treats Newark like their personal escape hatch to Europe at this point.

We're talking Luxembourg, Ljubljana, Ibiza, Marseille, Valencia, even the Azores. Olbia and Catania too, though funny enough, Delta already snuck into both of those via JFK after some kind of fan vote thing. So United's "first to market" claim has a little asterisk there.

They're also beefing up existing routes — new San Francisco to Tel Aviv service is coming back, Denver's finally getting nonstop Paris, and LA to Osaka is happening too. Brett over at Cranky Flier called this United's annual "big announcement" tradition, and yeah, at this point it basically is a tradition, like the Macy's parade but with A321XLRs instead of balloons.

Naturally, American wasn't about to let United have a whole week of headlines to itself. So they rolled out their own announcement — seven new international routes, three brand new cities. Porto, Reykjavik, and Vienna, all flying out of their Philadelphia hub.

Now, three new cities sounds modest next to United's eight. But that's kind of the American strategy lately — smaller, more surgical additions instead of United's fireworks show. Reykjavik's a smart pickup too, it's basically a mini-hub itself with all the connections onward into Europe.

Here's the thing about both of these announcements though — a lot of this is seasonal. Little asterisks everywhere. Summer-only, three-times-weekly, that kind of stuff. So don't go booking your Ibiza trip just yet, some of this doesn't start until next May.

But step back for a second, because this is really just United and American fighting over the same thing — people connecting through their hubs to get somewhere more interesting than Newark or Philly. Nobody's dying to spend a layover in either city, no offense to either city. The real prize is capturing that "I'm flying to Europe anyway, might as well go through you" traveler.

And honestly, this rivalry benefits us. More nonstops, more weird niche destinations, more options that didn't exist two years ago. Nobody was clamoring for nonstop Olbia service, but here we are, and somehow it's a whole competitive battleground now.

If you're the kind of person who tracks airline schedule changes for fun — first of all, hi, welcome to the club — this was a genuinely fun week to watch. Two of the biggest US carriers basically had a flex-off, and travelers are the ones who get the new nonstops out of it.
