Runway Briefing — United's Rough Day, Kirby's Chicago Prediction, and Frontier Plays Both Sides
Episode Summary: A United flight from LA to Newark turns into a two-stop odyssey after an engine issue forces a diversion to Vegas. United's CEO doubles down on his prediction that American will eventually bail on Chicago. Frontier makes Diamond status easier to reach while quietly gutting everyone else's perks. Plus, Boeing's engineers just said "no thanks" to the latest contract, and two air traffic controllers are getting fired over the LaGuardia crash.

Hey, welcome to Runway Briefing for Friday, August 28th. Let's get into it.

So, United Flight 1403. Supposed to be a pretty boring hop from LA to Newark. Instead, it turned into a whole thing.

Somewhere over the desert, the crew reports an engine issue and diverts into Las Vegas. Which, fine, that's exactly what you want a pilot to do — better safe airport than sorry. They land fine at Harry Reid, nobody's hurt.

But then it's hours on the ground while United scrambles to find a replacement plane. And get this — Simple Flying's headline says the flight changed aircraft and diverted twice. So passengers who booked a nonstop got basically a connecting-flight fever dream, minus the connecting flight. If you were on that plane, I hope you at least got status miles for emotional damage.

Next up — Scott Kirby is at it again. United's CEO gave an interview to Crain's Chicago Business doubling down on something he's been saying all year: American Airlines is eventually going to retreat from O'Hare.

His math — American lost about $1.1 billion in Chicago over the past year, and Kirby thinks that number only gets worse. His vision for what happens next? United turns O'Hare into a fortress hub, like Delta has in Atlanta, with a thousand daily flights and quintuple the international routes.

Now, is Kirby a totally neutral observer here? Not exactly. He's the guy who benefits most if American folds. But even Live and Let's Fly, who wrote this up, said they hope he's wrong — because a United monopoly at O'Hare is great for United and pretty rough for anyone who lives in Chicago and likes competitive fares.

Frontier's turn. They announced two things this week that sound like they're fighting each other, and honestly, they kind of are.

The good news — Elite Diamond, their top tier, just got easier to hit. Threshold dropped from 100,000 points down to 75,000. Nice.

The less good news, buried right next to it — Frontier is slashing mileage earning up to 90% for basic fares and non-cardholders, and stripping seat selection and change fee waivers from elites who don't buy up from the base fare. Even Platinum members are losing unlimited companion travel. So the top of the mountain got closer, but they moved the ladder. Classic.

Boeing's got a labor problem brewing. Engineers and technicians just voted down the company's latest contract offer, and Boeing's response was basically "well, we've got a contingency plan," which is corporate for "we were already expecting this."

A strike here would hit differently than the machinists strike a couple years back — these are the engineers, the people actually designing and certifying the planes. Given how much scrutiny Boeing's already under, the timing is not great. Then again, when has Boeing's timing ever been great.

Last one, and it's heavier. AirlineGeeks is reporting the FAA is moving to fire two air traffic controllers who reportedly left their shift early on the night of the LaGuardia crash. No word yet on exactly what role that played, but it tells you where the investigation is heading.

Combine that with the FAA still sitting on a chunk of the 2.8% raise Congress approved back in April, and you've got controllers working ten-hour shifts, six days a week, watching the agency drag its feet on both accountability and pay. Not exactly a recipe for a system that isn't stretched thin.
