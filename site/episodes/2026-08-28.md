Runway Briefing – United Goes on a Shopping Spree, American Tries to Keep Up
Episode Summary: United drops ten new international destinations at a splashy Newark event, American answers with three new cities of its own, and we talk about what it means when "new route" mostly means "more ways to leave New Jersey."

Hey, welcome to Runway Briefing for Friday, August 28th. Let's get into it.

So it's United's favorite time of year — the annual "look how big our map got" event. This year they held it in Newark, which, sure, fitting, because eight of their ten new destinations fly out of there.

Ten new international cities total, and eight of them aren't served by any other U.S. airline. That's actually a big deal — that's not just "we added a flight," that's "we're the only way to get there nonstop from America."

Most of it's landing in Europe. Luxembourg, Marseille, Ibiza, Valencia, a bunch of Italian spots like Olbia and Catania. A couple of those already get JFK service from Delta, but get this — only because fans literally voted for it. United just... decided.

San Francisco gets Okinawa, which is a genuinely interesting one, and Dulles picks up Toulouse. They're also beefing up existing routes — Denver to Paris, LA to Osaka, San Francisco back to Tel Aviv, Dulles to Milan.

Brett over at Cranky Flier called this United's "big announcement," and yeah, it earns the caps. Ten new cities in one swing is a lot. Basically if you live near Newark and have ever wanted to go to Ljubljana on a Thursday, 2026 is your year.

Okay, so American wasn't about to just stand there and clap. Same week, they rolled out their own announcement — seven new international routes, three brand new cities on the map: Porto, Reykjavik, and Vienna.

All three flying out of Philly, which makes sense, that's American's east coast door to Europe. It's a smaller swing than United's — three new cities versus United's eight unique ones — but it's not nothing. Reykjavik especially fills a gap, since American hasn't really had a strong Iceland play.

Here's the thing though. United announced this stuff at a big flashy event in Newark with a whole unveiling. American just kind of... put out a release. Same week, same news cycle, and United completely stole the room. Timing matters, and American's PR team is probably not thrilled about the calendar this month.

Bottom line, if you're a travel nerd, this is a genuinely fun week — two major U.S. carriers both went big on international growth at the same time, which almost never happens like this. If you're just trying to fly home for Thanksgiving on a budget airline, none of this affects you even a little bit.
