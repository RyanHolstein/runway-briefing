**Runway Briefing: United and American Go on a Route-Announcing Spree**

Episode Summary: United drops ten new international destinations, American answers with seven of its own, and Newark just became the center of the aviation universe.

---

If you've ever wanted to fly nonstop to Ibiza from New Jersey, today's your lucky day.

You're listening to Runway Briefing. I'm your host. Let's get into it.

So, United had its big annual route reveal this week, and it went big. Ten new international destinations, mostly small European cities you don't usually see on a US airline's map — Luxembourg, Ljubljana, Ibiza, Valencia, Marseille, that kind of thing. Eight of the ten aren't served by any other US carrier right now.

And here's the pattern — eight of those ten routes fly out of Newark. United's basically turning EWR into the gateway for every quirky European city you've ever wanted to visit. They're also adding some bigger routes too — Denver to Paris, LA to Osaka, San Francisco back to Tel Aviv.

Brett over at Cranky Flier broke this down in detail on the site earlier this week if you want the full route list, but the takeaway is simple: United's betting hard on Newark and on leisure travelers wanting direct access to secondary European cities instead of connecting through the usual hubs.

American wasn't about to let United have the whole week. They came out with seven new international routes of their own, adding three brand-new cities to the map — Porto, Reykjavik, and Vienna — all flying out of their Philadelphia hub.

It's a smaller move than United's, but it tells you the same story. Both airlines are chasing the same thing this summer — new leisure markets in Europe, launched from their biggest East Coast hubs. United's going deep from Newark, American's doing the same from Philly.

Why does this matter if you're not an aviation nerd? Because it means more direct flight options to places you'd normally have to connect through London or Paris to reach. Ibiza without a layover. Vienna without a layover. That's a real win for travelers, even if it's really about the airlines trying to out-flex each other for headlines.

Worth watching which of these routes actually stick around past next summer, though. A lot of these seasonal announcements quietly disappear a year later if the loads don't hold up. We'll keep an eye on it.
